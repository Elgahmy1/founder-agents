# Phase 1, step 3: Customer interviews (Mom Test)

Goal: Validate that freelancers with multiple clients find creating and sharing weekly status updates time‑consuming, error‑prone, or otherwise painful, and that they have tried or invested in workarounds.

## Who to talk to
- Freelancer who works with at least two active clients
- Regularly sends weekly (or similar cadence) status updates to clients
- Currently uses email, PDF/Word, Google Docs/Sheets, or a generic project‑management tool for those updates

## Questions (past behaviour, not opinions)
1. Can you walk me through the last time you had to send a weekly status update to a client? What format did you use and how did you create it?
2. How long did it take you from gathering the data to hitting send?
3. What tools or steps did you have to combine to produce that update (e.g., time‑tracking app, spreadsheet, copy‑paste, formatting)?
4. What was the most frustrating part of that process?
5. Did you try any other method or tool to make it easier? If so, what did you try and why did you stop using it?
6. Have you ever paid for a template, add‑on, or service to help with weekly reporting? If yes, what did you spend and what was the result?
7. When a client asks for a quick view of progress, how do you currently give it to them?
8. If you had a way to automatically generate a client‑visible dashboard from your time logs, what would you do differently?

## Avoid
- Would you buy a tool that does this?
- Do you think this is a good idea?
- How much would you be willing to pay?
- Would you use a dashboard instead of email?
- Do you think freelancers need a dashboard?
- Any hypothetical "what if" questions about future behavior

Problem counts as confirmed if: The interviewee describes a specific recent incident where they spent measurable time (e.g., >15 minutes) manually assembling a weekly update using multiple tools, expresses clear frustration, and mentions having already invested time or money in a workaround (e.g., custom template, paid add‑on, script).
