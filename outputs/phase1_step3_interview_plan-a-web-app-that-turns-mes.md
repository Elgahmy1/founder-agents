# Phase 1, step 3: Customer interviews (Mom Test)

Goal: Validate whether undergraduate STEM students experience significant pain from having to manually reorganize lecture PDFs/slides into concise, exam‑ready summaries and create practice questions, and whether they already invest time or money in workarounds.

## Who to talk to
- Currently enrolled in an undergraduate STEM program
- Uses digital lecture materials (PDF, PPT, DOCX) for courses
- Has prepared for at least one midterm or final exam in the past semester
- Has spent noticeable time (e.g., >2 hours) manually summarizing notes or creating practice questions

## Questions (past behaviour, not opinions)
1. Can you walk me through the last time you prepared for a midterm/final in a STEM class? What materials did you start with?
2. How did you turn those lecture PDFs or slides into study‑ready notes? What steps did you take?
3. How long did that process take you from opening the file to feeling ready to study?
4. Did you use any tools or services to help you summarize or generate practice questions? Which ones and why?
5. What was the biggest frustration or bottleneck in that process?
6. Did you end up paying for any help (e.g., tutoring, paid summary service, buying a study guide) to fill the gaps? How much did it cost?
7. After you finished, how confident did you feel that your notes covered the exam material?
8. If you had a way to get an exam‑focused summary and practice questions automatically, what would you have done differently that week?

## Avoid
- Would you buy a tool that creates exam‑ready summaries?
- Do you think this is a good idea?
- How much would you be willing to pay for such a service?
- Would you use it if it existed?
- Do you like the concept of AI‑generated study material?

Problem counts as confirmed if: The interviewee describes a specific recent exam‑preparation episode where they had to manually reorganize lecture PDFs/slides and create practice questions, and they report having spent at least a couple of hours or paid for a workaround (e.g., tutoring, paid summary service, premium software) to address the problem.
