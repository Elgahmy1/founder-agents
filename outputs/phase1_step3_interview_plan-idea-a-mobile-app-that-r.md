# Phase 1, step 3: Customer interviews (Mom Test)

Goal: Validate whether office managers/HR professionals experience a real, recurring problem with employee hydration that existing low‑effort solutions (posters, occasional emails, generic wellness programs) don’t adequately solve, indicating a need for a digital hydration dashboard.

## Who to talk to
- Works in an office environment with at least 10 employees
- Responsible for employee wellness, HR, or office facilities
- Has noticed employees being low on water or energy during the workday
- Has tried any hydration‑related reminder (posters, emails, apps, water stations) in the past
- Can describe a specific recent incident related to hydration

## Questions (past behaviour, not opinions)
1. Can you tell me about the last time you noticed employees seemed thirsty, low on energy, or mentioned dehydration during the workday?
2. What did you do at that moment to try to fix the situation?
3. How did you come up with that solution (e.g., printed something, sent an email, bought a tool) and how long did it take?
4. Did you spend any money or allocate any budget for that solution? If so, how much?
5. What was the outcome – did employees drink more water or feel better?
6. What other methods have you tried in the past to encourage people to stay hydrated at work?
7. When you consider adding a new wellness tool, who needs to approve it and what criteria do they use?
8. How much screen space or intranet integration would IT typically allow for a new widget?

## Avoid
- Would you buy a hydration dashboard?
- Do you think a digital widget would be useful?
- How much would you be willing to pay?
- Would you use something like this?
- What features would you like to see?
- Is this a good idea?
- Can you imagine using a hydration widget?

Problem counts as confirmed if: The interviewee describes a specific recent incident where employees were noticeably dehydrated, explains that they took concrete action (e.g., created/ordered posters, sent targeted emails, purchased a reminder app), and mentions the time or money spent on that workaround while still feeling the solution was insufficient or cumbersome.
