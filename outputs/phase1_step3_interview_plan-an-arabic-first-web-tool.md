# Phase 1, step 3: Customer interviews (Mom Test)

Goal: Validate whether Egyptian freelancers who primarily speak Arabic experience significant pain writing English proposals on platforms like Upwork/Fiverr, and whether they already spend time or money on workarounds.

## Who to talk to
- Egyptian nationality or resident
- Primary language Arabic
- Active freelancer on Upwork, Fiverr or similar international platform
- Has written at least one English proposal in the past 3 months

## Questions (past behaviour, not opinions)
1. Can you walk me through the last proposal you wrote for an Upwork/Fiverr job? What was the project and how did you start?
2. What steps did you take to turn your idea into an English proposal? (e.g., manual translation, using a tool, asking a friend)
3. Did you use any software or services (ChatGPT, translation apps, hired editors, templates) for that proposal? Which ones?
4. How long did the whole proposal‑writing process take you from start to submission?
5. Did you spend any money on tools, freelancers, or services to help with that proposal? If so, how much and what for?
6. What part of the process felt most frustrating or time‑consuming?
7. After you submitted the proposal, what was the outcome? Did you get the job, get feedback, or have to rewrite?
8. When you hit a language barrier, what have you tried to overcome it (e.g., Facebook group templates, asking peers, using generic English AI)?
9. Have you ever looked for explanations of English phrasing in Arabic? What did you do when you couldn’t find them?

## Avoid
- Would you buy a tool that writes proposals in English for you?
- How much would you be willing to pay for an Arabic‑first proposal writer?
- Do you think a product like this would be useful?
- If we built this, would you use it?
- Would you recommend it to other freelancers?

Problem counts as confirmed if: The interviewee describes a specific recent proposal they struggled with, explains that they spent measurable time (e.g., >2 hours) or money (e.g., paid for translation, a freelancer, or a premium tool) to overcome the language barrier, and expresses clear frustration about the lack of Arabic guidance.

## Outreach draft (queued for founder approval; the founder sends it personally)
